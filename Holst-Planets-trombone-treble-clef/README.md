# Holst's Planets trombone parts, transposed up a ninth into treble clef

## Purpose

This set of "brass band clef" trombone parts has been created to facilitate brass band tenor trombonists who wish to play Holst's Planets.  It is one of the sets of parts required to resolve the problems described below.

## Problem statement

This table describes the clefs used for the low brass parts in the original published edition of Holst's Planets.

| Number | Planet | Tenor tuba | Bass tuba | Trombone 1 | Trombone 2 | Bass trombone |
|---------|---------|---------|---------|---------|---------|---------|
| 1 | Mars | brass band | bass | tenor | tenor | bass |
| 2 | Venus | tacet | tacet | tacet | tacet | tacet |
| 3 | Mercury | tacet | tacet | tacet | tacet | tacet |
| 4 | Jupiter | brass band | bass | tenor and bass | tenor | bass |
| 5 | Saturn | tacet | bass | tenor and bass | tenor and bass | bass |
| 6 | Uranus | brass band | bass | tenor and bass | tenor and bass | bass | |
| 7 | Neptune | tacet | tacet | bass | bass | bass |

Low brass players are not uniform in what instruments we can play, nor in what clefs we can read.
- A brass band musician might be competent playing both valved instruments and trombone, yet be unable to read bass clef.
- An orchestral, marching band, or jazz trombonist might read only bass clef and want to take their own bass clef printouts.
- A marching band euphonist might be unable to read the original "brass band clef" tenor tuba part and wish they had brought their own bass clef printout.

## Glossary

The columns for instruments contain the clefs used in the printed part for each movement.
- "tacet" means that this instrument has nothing to play during this movement.
- "brass band" refers to the British brass band convention, established by Mr Adolphe Sax, of [writing saxhorn music in treble clef no matter the size or key of the instrument][4].  This differs from the US marching band convention of writing low brass parts in concert pitch bass clef.  As a result of this, concert middle C is written as fourth line D for a brass band trombonist, euphonist, or baritone saxhorn player.
- "tenor" and "bass" refer to the orchestral convention of representing trombone music in concert pitch with a clef that apparently changes at the whim of the composer.
